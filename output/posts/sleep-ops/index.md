Sleep is so important that I wanted to learn more about what I could do to have the best sleep possible. This post shares some of my research about sleep, and how I performed a sleep study to try out different outfits to figure out the best sleepwear that worked for me.

### Benefits of Sleep

Sleep allows your body (energy consumption reduction) and mind (brain cell restoration) to recharge. Without enough sleep your brain cannot function properly and impairs your ability to concentrate, think clearly, and to recall memories. Most adults require seven to nine hours of sleep a day. During sleep, your muscles recover, growth hormone is produced, and protein synthesis occurs (if you consumed protein prior to sleep).

### Best Practices

With the internet there is so much information out there about sleep and sometimes it is conflicting. Here are some best practices that I have compiled that work for me.

- Go to bed and get up at the same time every day

- Right when you get up get sunlight/bright light for 2-10 minutes as it helps set your internal clock

- Don't oversleep as it may set your body clock to a different cycle

- Avoid bright lights late at night as it messes up your internal clock

- Do not look at computer or phone screens late at night

- Only have sex (in marriage) and sleep in bed - do not do other activities there

- Be clean (some sources say to take a warm bath) and have clean sheets

- In particular I change out my pillow case every couple days which helps to limit allergens building up

- Don't exercise right before bed unless that is the only time you can

- Avoid alcohol or caffeine at night

- Avoid taking sleeping pills as they may work temporarily but in the long term will cause disturbed sleep patterns

- Try having a nice scent in the air before bed

- Have the temperature around 67 degrees Fahrenheit in the bedroom

- Some sources suggest having white noise (a small fan running) or soft background music to help you to relax and encourage sleep

### Sleep Study Approach/Tactics

My hypothesis prior to setting about with a sleep study was that I could try out different sleep wear and find the one that gave me the most comfortable sleep. In my sleep study I wanted to push my normal sleepwear boundaries and boldly go where I had never gone before. For me that was wearing socks. I have very rarely worn socks when I go to sleep other than when I was in the Army in the field. For some people they have such cold feet that they must wear socks and sweats to bed. But for me I am always warm and get way too hot in bed.

**Sleep Journal** - Take notes from each night of sleep to figure out how long I sleep, how many times I wake up, how many times I go to pee, etc…

**Try Pillow App** \- There are numerous apps that do this. I tried the sleep monitoring, but did not try the alarm function which is supposed to wake you up at your ideal time.

**Try different outfits** - Long sleeve pajama set - my current go to configuration. Would like to try out different combinations to see if I sleep better.

### Key Learnings/Findings

Individual results may vary but this is what I found out that worked best for me.

**Socks** are a must!! Lightweight, loosely fitting, just over ankle seems best (my Nike workout little socks seem perfect, maybe a tiny bit looser ones would be good, but those work nicely). I never wore socks before and was very skeptical as I do not like anything on my feet. But there actually is a lot of research out there that supports that wearing socks actually helps you sleep.

> Warming up your feet and hands can initiate a process called vasodilation (widening of the blood vessels) which can help prepare your brain for sleep.
> 
> According to the National Sleep Foundation

**Boxer shorts** that are comfortable but not too baggy. Doesn’t need the fly opening. Are there boxers that do not have a fly?

**Long sleeved pajama top or shirt** - pretty close tie between the two. For some reason I like having a collar as that little bit more fabric keeps my comfortable and cozy.

**Pima Cotton Fabric** - Soft and breathes well this is the go to fabric for me.

### Final Thoughts

There are many other options to try out in the future including introducing eye cover, a hat, or gloves into the mix. For me I don't think a hat would work as I would be too hot and it would fall off, gloves maybe, an eye cover probably would fall off as well.

I encourage you to try out your own sleep study and find out what works best for you. For me it was well worth it as I have dialed in a sleeping outfit that is the most comfortable for me.
