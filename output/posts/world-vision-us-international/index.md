This post explores why I applied to work at World Vision, the experiences and relationships I developed while working there, and why I moved on.

## Why World Vision?

Back in 2010 while I had been working at Microsoft in Redmond, Washington for over a decade I started feeling like I was only employee #11487 to the company I had dedicated so many years of my life to. I was facing re-org number six at the time. For those who don't know what a re-org is, it is something that happens at large corporations where the employees are laid off, and the ones who survive are often moved into different teams and organizations. The idea behind this is that it stimulates productivity and efficiency. What it always did for me was give me a bad feeling in the pit of my stomach.

I started looking at job opportunities outside of Microsoft that perhaps might better align with my life goals and dreams. I looked into what it would take to be a police officer (which I'll cover in a different post) and into Non Profit Organizations that were serving the Lord in demonstrable ways reaching out to the poor and suffering in the world. At a Casting Crowns concert our family was introduced to World Vision and signed up to sponsor a child in Africa.

https://youtu.be/q-WYb0T512g

I saw an opening at World Vision US (WVUS), in Federal Way, Washington as a technology director and went through the interview process and the final interview was with the CTO and Director of development. In the interview I shared my testimony and tried to understand better what the position was about and they were curious about how I would recommend they approach their technology strategy and organize their business. It ended up that I was not selected for that position.

I applied a second time in the summer of 2011 at WVUS for a Program Manager position in the Marketing, Internet Business Unit, Client Engagement Team and was accepted. It was a big culture shock going from Microsoft where I had my own office and managed international teams and massive budgets. I was given a cubicle in an open area with seven other people and my first task was to help develop an internet development guide for the organization to capture standards and best practices. The people were all so friendly and we went to chapel every Wednesday and the company was like one big family.

While at WVUS I helped to completely overhaul their internet site and worked with the Director of Mobile Technology to deliver an iOS child sponsorship app. After almost two years at WVUS I was asked to consider a position at World Vision International (WVI) from a WVUS colleague that had jumped ship to WVI and jumped at the opportunity.

The position I accepted at WVI was as a Global Solutions Manager, in the Digital Marketing team and my job allowed me to travel to many of the international World Vision offices to help them with their digital marketing solutions and strategies. These were amazingly rich times where I was able to serve alongside brothers and sisters in Christ who were so passionate to help the most vulnerable children in need around the world.

## Moving on

I soon found out that re-orgs were not just a thing that happened in for profit corporations but also happened quite often at global non profit organizations. Shortly after joining the team we were re-orged under new management where I successfully made the cut and then our director left the organization and we were under a completely new management. There were continued efforts to try to cut budgets and move teams offshore to cheaper locales. In this instance it was to move the whole service organization to the Philippines.
