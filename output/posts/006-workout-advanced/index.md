### Performance Over Aesthetic

> If you have amazing athletic performance, your aesthetic looks great because of performance. We never train for aesthetic, we train for athleticism.
> 
> Simon Waterson, Movie Health & Fitness Advisor

Simon Waterson trains actors to be ready for their demanding roles. Most notably Waterson trained Daniel Craig for his James Bond movies. Waterson trains his clients like an athlete and puts performance over looks. When training an actor he focuses on the movements that are relevant to the movie. The looks are the after product. The overall focus is on being healthy, and being able to make it through the production. Speed, agility, and recovery were emphasized for the James Bond movies. Simon tries to replicate the movements in the gym, then takes them to the stunt room, then to the stage. Physical & mental fatigue are challenges for actors who have to deal with grueling shooting schedules of 16 hour+ days.

Diet guidance from Waterson focuses on what will make people feel happy/comfortable. A little more plant based. He doesn’t cut out food groups. Waterson's maxims are good gut health = great absorption and he focuses on anti-inflammatory foods and foods that provide great energy. His diet programs are never about deprivation.

### Progressive Overload

![](images/G8dSxEvZdo0TIuIvSOfwNMIlC2AlxGb0mnmfzLVo2YYczrMrhSrRqpS-Nb9BFl3IOkHZGXtwWSAqKazo99TGpZlXNdxSKzI9P5qbzaSbvOrXwaCanSrG4R_dPdFhAq2taSb1zCnPUQ=w2400)

There are a few key terms and concepts that are critical to a deeper understanding of how to train your body. Progressive overload is defined as:

- A bodybuilding principle that involves continually increasing the demands on the musculoskeletal system to continually make gains in muscle size, strength, and endurance.

> To get bigger and stronger you have to continually make your muscles work harder than they are used to. Not just more weight but can be more reps or shorter rest.

    **Methods to achieve Progressive Overload**:

- Lifting the same load for increased distance (range of motion)

- Lifting the same load and volume with better form, more control, and less effort (efficiency)

- Lifting the same load for more reps (volume)

- Lifting heavier loads (intensity of load)

- Lifting the same load and volume with less rest time in between sets (density)

- Lifting a load with more speed and acceleration (intensity of effort)

- Doing more work in the same amount of time (density)

- Doing the same work in less amount of time (density)

- Doing more sets with the same load and reps (volume)

- Lifting the same load and volume more often throughout the week (frequency)

- Doing the same work while losing body mass (increased relative volume)

- Lifting the same load and volume and then extending the set past technical failure with forced reps, negatives, drop sets, static holds, rest pause, partial reps, or post-exhaustion (intensity of effort)

### Periodization

![](images/gGFNHixiSOU-brG1B9mPGbgTDoHyaWsEZegiufcyNm0krVN7QKsGNJOOdsUihW3x24wutU7L1pp7m5lk0ssO7RG5yH1omaM63D12vr4aDpj8lbpJcNJ02F92dGG78D2psGZoKWLV3A=w2400)

When it is time to change up your workout regimen try out periodization. Periodization is defined as:

- The gradual cycling of specificity, intensity, and volume of training to achieve peak levels of strength. (**Training Volume:** Is your total workload per exercise, session, and week. Simple equation: Sets x Reps x Load)

> Periodization is a helpful strategy for organizing your long-term training plan to cylce different levels of intensity from week to week to keep your body guessing.

**Training Volume Schemes for Targeted Goals**

| Training emphasis | Intensity | Repetitions | Sets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Muscular endurance** | <=67% | 10-25 | \>=3 |
| **Hypertrophy** (Pump) | 67-85% | 6-12 | \>=3 |
| **Muscular strength** (Explosive Power) | \>=85% | <=6 | \>=3 |
| **Muscular power - multiple rep event** | 75-85% | 3-5 | 3-6 |
| **Muscular power - single rep event** (PR) | 80-90% | 1-2 | 3-6 |

**Simple Method To Determine your 1RM**

- In order to use the training volume schemes in the above table you first have to determine your one repetition maximum or 1RM. This is the maximum weight per exercise that you can lift for one repetition. It is important to know this as you can then determine the amount of weight you need to lift to hit your desired intensity/repetition to hit your training volume emphasis.

- It is dangerous to do this and can lead to injury so often people use the following equation/ratios to help determine the right number of repetitions at what weight is appropriate.
    - Your 5RM is roughly equivalent to 85-87.5% of your 1RM
    
    - Your 3RM to 87.5-90%
    
    - Your 2RM to 90-92.5%

### Supplements

- Multi-vitamin - daily

- Fish oil

    - Have to take daily to build up in your system

### Different Tools & Techniques

[![](images/006-workout2-16-9.jpg)](https://glennkelly006.wordpress.com/2020/10/03/006-workout/)

**Resistance bands vs. free weights**. With free weights the initial part of a movement is difficult and then once you get it moving and it is at the top of the movement it typically doesn't provide much resistance or workout. With resistance bands it actually gets harder the more you stretch the band. It is not meant to completely replace free weights but rather is a good way to vary your workouts. A friend of mine that uses bands regularly finds that it helps to get at the deeper muscle layer and he incorporates repetitions with the bands in between free weight exercises.

**Blood Flow Restriction** (BFR) training. This is an advanced technique of restricting the blood flow to a body part like your arms or legs to put more stress on your muscles. I have purchased bands and incorporate them during my deload week. I have found a lot of muscle soreness the day following so it seems to work great. BFR training can also be used as a finishing move. Guidance is that it works more for isolation exercises. If you are interested in learning more I found this is a good resource: [Your Complete Guide To Blood Flow Restriction Training!](https://www.bodybuilding.com/content/your-complete-guide-to-blood-flow-restriction-training.html)

**Foam Roller** research seems to indicate that doing this prior to workout on areas that are sore stimulates circulation and reduces the pain. Doing the foam roller after is also advised to aid the lactic acid being worked out of the muscles and aids in speeding recovery.

### Professional Guidance

![](images/m3X2aRIr8_stNQJrcNaNEUX6Gz8t7iT7hqo0MdyFl_5pzFrX8xPiszx7-GRabFS-Q9Dj89WBwDppOZh7AjKiYumqHh2cub_fpP2aF6wB2EZ7skVfzCBJ3lVmHhuCcgiZ-2V-lVOsgQ=w2400)

- It is great to get professional guidance and input and I have found it to be invaluable. I shared my goals and workout plan with a friend of mine that is a certified personal trainer.
    - She liked the exercises that I had (in particular that I had the core of squats, deadlift, and bench).
    
    - She recommended to **not** focus on Muscular endurance as I had been but rather to achieve my fitness goals to try Periodization. For me this meant to not focus on Muscular Endurance but rather build a program which cycled through the following phases: Strength --> Power --> Deload

- After incorporating periodization into my routine I had another conversation with my Certified Personal Trainer friend sharing my top two goals which are shared below along with her excellent guidance:
    - Goal #1 Reduce Body fat to 10%
        - Cut calories down to 1,600-1,800/day
        
        - Hit my macros, in particular must hit my protein goals daily (at least 30g of protein per meal)
    
    - Goal #2 Increase body dimensions in areas to be proportional
        - Add calories to build at 2,000-2,200/day. And not get to over 15% body fat in the building phase.
        
        - Have to improve strength and lift more weight to see the improvements I’m looking for in terms of growth
        
        - Do not remove my lower body workout exercises but to emphasize growth in a particular body part I would need to add more targeted exercises for that area of the body. Can superset or do opposing muscle (push/pull) workout to put more demand on that particular area of the body.
    
    - Additionally I shared with her that I want to be more flexible and she shared some tips on a stretching routine to help target the areas where I needed the most work.
