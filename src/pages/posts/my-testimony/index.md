> Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away.
> 
> Matthew 24:35

My life is a testimony to the truth of God’s word. I was born in the state of Maine, USA to a Japanese mother and to my father who was a career Air Force officer. After several military relocations my dad retired and our family settled in sunny Southern California. In school growing up I was taught, and wholeheartedly accepted, that man’s words were truth. When I was a teenager I ventured to read the Bible on a dare from Christian friends. This dare began my journey toward accepting God’s word as the source of truth and receiving Christ as my savior.

My parents taught me to be an honest, hardworking, and productive man. I was not raised “religiously,” and our family did not attend church.

In the public school system I was taught evolution and that there was no God, accepting all of this as fact without question. Thankfully God had His perfect plan and put many Christian friends in my path while growing up. I would argue for hours with these friends about the meaning of life and the non-existence of God. They would respectfully provide their perspective, encourage me to read the Bible, and invite me to church. Once in a while I would humor them, but I was firmly entrenched in secular humanist thinking.

> If you haven't read the Bible for yourself I dare you to.

Proving I had no fear of this fictional book filled with superstition and folklore, I read the entire Bible in a year. It was nothing like I thought it would be. I certainly didn’t understand everything in the Bible, but gradually God’s word was beginning to work within me and would be instrumental in the next adventure I undertook. I had always dreamed of being a valiant soldier who served his country, so I joined the US Army. During that time in my life, I began earnestly crying out to God for help as the situations I was in were pushing me to my limits.

> I would pray to God “Keep me alive today,” and “Give me courage to face the challenges that this day will bring.”

I placed my trust in God and He kept me safe. Over the years I wanted to know more about who God was and longed to develop a deeper relationship with him. After serving in the US Army for four years, I went back home to be with my dad as he struggled through serious health issues. God was faithful to me during my Army experience and by His grace I was ready to take the next step.

I started attending Saddleback Church regularly and committed my life to Christ in the summer of 1997, making a public confession of my faith and commitment to Jesus Christ as my Lord and savior through baptism. Since then the Lord has blessed me in many ways. One of the immediate changes that I noticed was in my heart. Up to that point in my life I would characterize my personality as being like the Star Trek character, Mr. Spock, who felt no emotion and was ruled by logic. After committing myself to Christ, I felt things more deeply than ever before and had compassion for others.

I’m thankful that my life journey for truth and purpose didn’t end with following man’s words as truth, or reading the Bible and not understanding it, or just with one act of obedience years ago to accept the Lord as my savior and be baptized. My greatest joy now is the continual growth I experience daily in my relationship with the Lord. At home my wife and I encourage one another daily to live a life that glorifies the Lord and do all that we can to live out our lives as a testimony of the Lord to our two daughters. As members of a local body of believers we continue to grow in our understanding of the Lord through studying His word, praying for one another, and performing acts of service in our church family and local community. God’s word continues to be a guide in my life and will truly be the only thing that will remain and not pass away.
